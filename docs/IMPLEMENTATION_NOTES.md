# Implementation notes

What was actually implemented, and every deviation from `GAZEBO_SIM_GUIDE.md`. The paper text must
match this file (guide section 2.6). Items marked **PAPER** need a sentence in the manuscript.
Items marked **OPEN** need a decision from the author.

## 1. Platform

- **Deviation.** The guide prefers ROS 2 Jazzy + Gazebo Harmonic (Ubuntu 24.04). This machine runs
  Ubuntu 26.04, so the simulation uses **ROS 2 Lyrical + Gazebo (gz-sim) 10.4.0**, native, via `ros_gz`.
  Chosen by the author on 2026-10-08. Not Gazebo Classic. **PAPER**: state the versions.
- Robot: TurtleBot 3 Waffle from `ROBOTIS-GIT/turtlebot3_simulations` tag 2.3.7
  (commit `9be186fb03d84ed4f293e5c0db71d8c05bbc91f3`), modified by `sim/models/build_robot_model.py`:
  lidar removed; the stock 1920x1080 camera replaced by the sensor head below; ground-truth publisher added.
- Rendering: headless (`gz sim -s --headless-rendering`), ogre2, NVIDIA GPU.
- Host quirk: `ros_gz_bridge` deadlocks at start-up unless `LTTNG_UST_REGISTER_TIMEOUT=0` (set in `env.sh`).

## 2. Sensors (guide 4.2)

| Sensor | Resolution | Rate | H-FOV | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RGB | 640x480 | 30 Hz | 1.047 rad | Gaussian noise, sigma 0.007 (of full scale) |
| Depth | 640x480 | 30 Hz | 1.047 rad | float32 metres, exported as 16-bit PNG in millimetres |
| Thermal | 320x240 | 30 Hz | 1.047 rad | 16-bit, 0.005 K per count |

All three share one pose (link `camera_rgb_frame`, 0.069, -0.047, 0.107 m in the robot frame).
Measured on the tuning sequence: the three streams carry identical time stamps, no dropped frames.

- **Thermal intrinsics.** gz-sim 10 publishes a thermal `camera_info` computed from a default 45 degree
  vertical FOV (fx = 289.7 px), which does not match the rendered image. Edge alignment of thermal against
  depth frames shows the rendered thermal image has the same field of view as RGB/depth (best scale 1.00).
  The exporter therefore uses the RGB intrinsics scaled by 0.5 (fx = 277.19 px) and keeps the published
  values in `camera.yaml` for the record.
- **Thermal sensor noise is not simulated by Gazebo.** A `<noise>` element on the thermal camera has no
  effect in gz-sim 10 (verified: values stay on the quantisation grid). **OPEN**: add seeded Gaussian noise
  (for example NETD 50 mK) at replay time, or state that the simulated thermal stream is noise-free.
- **Warm-up frames.** The first thermal frame is rendered before the sensor resolution is applied and is
  wrong (half temperature). The exporter drops all frames stamped before 1.0 s; the robot stands still for
  the first 2 s of every route.

## 3. Thermal scene model (guide 4.3) - PAPER

- **Simulator limitation: temperature quantisation.** gz-rendering passes every heat source through an
  8-bit buffer spanning 0 .. 65535 x resolution kelvin. Rendered temperatures are therefore quantised to
  257 x resolution. At the usual 0.01 K/count that is 2.57 K; this project uses **0.005 K/count, giving a
  1.285 K step and a 327.7 K ceiling**. All scene temperatures are placed on that grid and kept <= 327 K
  (hot pipes, radiators and machinery are cooler than the 320-350 K suggested in the guide).
- **Heat-signature textures are not used.** They are quantised the same way, and in gz-sim 10 they ignore a
  non-default sensor resolution (objects render at the minimum temperature).
- **Thermal structure comes from "thermal decals"**: thin uniform-temperature patches on walls, floor,
  ceiling and machinery (studs/joists, pipe and cable runs behind the surface, panels, small hot spots),
  placed by a seeded generator. They carry a visibility flag that the RGB and depth cameras mask out, so
  they exist only in the thermal image and thermal structure is independent of visible texture.
  Consequence: simulated thermal images are piecewise constant (no smooth gradients).
- People are primitives (cylinder + sphere) at 300-308 K; no Fuel models, so worlds are self-contained.
- Worlds, textures and routes are generated by `sim/worldgen/build_worlds.py` (seeded, deterministic).

## 4. Worlds, routes, recording (guide 4.1, 4.4, 4.5)

- `office_loop` (ring corridor, closed loop), `corridor_tunnel` (26 m, low RGB texture on the walls, out and
  back), `industrial_hall` (20 x 14 m, machine island, closed loop); `office_loop_tune` is the tuning sequence
  (other start corner, opposite direction, 18 m).
- Route driver: scripted `cmd_vel` controller with ground-truth feedback (turn in place, drive straight at
  0.22 m/s). Runs are repeatable to about 0.2 s over 88 s, not bit-identical; each sequence is recorded once
  and every configuration replays that recording.
- Recorded to MCAP rosbag, then exported to a frame dataset (`harness/export_sequence.py`): PNG frames,
  `frames.csv`, `camera.yaml`, ground truth in TUM format. **Replay reads the exported frames, not the bag**,
  so no ROS process runs during SLAM/energy measurement. Depth is quantised to 1 mm on export.
- Ground truth: true pose of `base_footprint` from Gazebo at 50 Hz, transformed to the camera optical frame
  and interpolated to frame stamps (`groundtruth.txt`).

## 5. Degradation (guide 5) - PAPER

- Applied at replay to the **RGB stream only**. Thermal is never degraded. This is a modelling assumption
  (thermal imaging treated as insensitive to smoke and darkness), not a measured property.
- Depth is also left clean. **OPEN**: a real RGB-D depth sensor (active IR) degrades in smoke; currently
  depth stays valid inside the degradation window, which favours every configuration equally but makes
  the scenario easier than reality.
- Profiles are defined as fractions of the sequence duration (`config/profiles.yaml`): onset at 30 %, 5 s
  ramps, hold for 30 %. Noise is seeded by (profile seed, frame index): bit-identical across runs (unit-tested).

## 6. Controller (guide 7)

- Eq. 1 and Eq. 2 as written. Q can be slightly negative; it is clipped at 0 only for the weights.
- **Thermal-priority rule - PAPER.** Fig. 2's "increase thermal weight" is implemented as: while
  `Q_RGB < tau_Q` and `Q_TH >= tau_Q`, `omega_TH = max(omega_TH from Eq. 2, 0.7)`. The value 0.7
  (`thermal_priority_weight`) is a new parameter that the paper must state; hysteresis band 0.02 on tau_Q.
- Levels: downgrade at the threshold, upgrade at threshold + 0.02 (fraction of E0), minimum dwell 2 s,
  one level per transition.
- **Sensor activation u_m.** No rule in the guide switches a sensor off at run time, so u_m is constant per
  configuration (1 for the modalities the configuration uses). **OPEN** if the paper claims otherwise.
- `V_ref` and the thermal temperature window are calibrated on clear frames of the tuning sequence
  (`harness/calibrate.py` -> `config/calibration.yaml`).

## 7. SLAM integration (guide 6) - PAPER

- ORB-SLAM3 commit `4452a3c4` (v1.0), **RGB-D mode**, SE(3) alignment for ATE. Depth settings are those of
  ORB-SLAM3's TUM RGB-D examples (baseline 0.08 m, ThDepth 40, i.e. close points within 3.2 m).
- **Section 6.2 (weighted feature pooling) is implemented; the 6.3 fallback is not used.** Details and the
  full diff: `orbslam3_patch/ORBSLAM3_CHANGES.md`. In short: two ORB extractors with budgets
  `n_m = round(omega_m * n_total)` (minimum 50), keypoints pooled into one frame, a modality byte appended to
  every descriptor, descriptor distance forced to "no match" across modalities.
- **Thermal is resized to 640x480 (bilinear) before extraction**, so thermal keypoints share the RGB pixel
  grid, the depth image and the scale pyramid. Features are therefore not extracted at the native 320x240.
- **"Reduced" loop closure, quantitatively:** place recognition for one keyframe in three; after a loop
  correction, pose-graph optimisation only (no global bundle adjustment). "Off": no detection.
- **N_m(t)** = tracked inliers of modality m in the previous frame / that frame's budget for m. If the previous
  frame was not tracked, detected keypoints are used; the first frame has no feedback (N = 0). The column
  `n_source` in `frames.csv` says which.
- **Replay is paced at the recorded 30 Hz** (sleep until each frame's time stamp; no frame dropping if
  processing falls behind, the lag is logged). Image loading, RGB degradation and thermal 8-bit conversion
  happen before the timed section; the timed (energy-measured) section contains the quality indicators,
  the scheduler and ORB-SLAM3.
- **Two trajectories per run:** `trajectory_tum.txt` (ORB-SLAM3's final trajectory after all optimisation;
  primary ATE) and `trajectory_online_tum.txt` (pose reported at each frame while tracking was OK).
- **Tracking loss and ATE.** After a loss ORB-SLAM3 starts a new map with a new origin. ATE is still computed
  with one SE(3) alignment over all poses, so runs with a loss get a very large ATE (metres). Losses and
  re-initialisations are counted separately in `summary.json`. **OPEN**: how the paper reports such runs
  (for example ATE with failures counted, plus tracked fraction).
- ORB-SLAM3 needs more than 500 keypoints to (re)initialise an RGB-D map; with the Level 2 budget of 500 a
  map cannot be re-created after a loss while Level 2 is active.

## 8. Observations that affect what the paper can claim

Development runs on the tuning sequence, one repeat each, no energy measurement, machine busy with a
Gazebo recording at the time. Not results; they show what to expect.

| Profile | Configuration | ATE RMSE (m) | Tracked | Losses |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| clear | static_rgb | 0.054 | 100 % | 0 |
| clear | static_thermal | 0.026 | 100 % | 0 |
| clear | static_fusion | 0.020 | 100 % | 0 |
| fog_severe | static_rgb | 4.78 | 68 % | 1 (new map) |
| fog_severe | static_fusion | 0.013 | 100 % | 0 |
| fog_severe | adaptive | 0.021 | 100 % | 0 |

- **The simulated thermal stream is an idealised sensor.** It is noise-free, never degraded and made of
  sharp-edged patches, and thermal-only SLAM is *more* accurate than RGB-only in clear conditions
  (0.026 m vs 0.054 m). With thermal this good, `static_thermal` and `static_fusion` may match or beat
  `adaptive` on accuracy in every scenario, which would leave the adaptive weighting with nothing to show.
  **OPEN (decide before tuning and freezing):** make thermal realistic (sensor noise, lower contrast,
  a thermal-degradation scenario) or state the idealisation as a limitation.
- **Stock ORB-SLAM3 RGB-D drift.** Unmodified `rgbd_tum` on the clear tuning sequence gives ATE 0.059 m over
  18 m; 0.015 m after Sim(3) alignment (scale 1.013). The guide expected "a few cm". The error is drift along
  the route, not a frame or time-stamp offset (z error 2 mm; path-length ratio 0.9965).
- **The blur indicator B responds to noise as if it were sharpness.** At peak `lowlight` B_RGB = 0.00, and
  B_RGB is lower for `fog_severe` (0.39) than for `fog_moderate` (0.67). Q_RGB still falls in all three
  profiles because N collapses, but B alone is not a reliable degradation indicator under noise.
- **Initial thresholds do not fit the measured Q range.** With tracked-inlier N, clear-condition means on the
  tuning sequence are Q_RGB = 0.45 and Q_TH = 0.23 (N is about 0.4, since only part of the budget is tracked).
  With the initial tau_Q = 0.45 RGB counts as degraded half the time in clear conditions and thermal is never
  "adequate", so the thermal-priority rule never fires. tau_Q (and possibly alpha/beta/gamma) must be tuned
  on the tuning sequence in M7.
- Thermal quality also varies with the scene: fewer thermal features when the robot faces a wall at short range.
- `fog_moderate` (peak 0.5) already removes about 90 % of detected RGB keypoints; the profile names
  understate the severity. Profiles are part of the experiment design and should be fixed before evaluation.

## 9. Energy measurement (guide 7.4) - first measurements, 2026-10-08

- RAPL package counter (`intel-rapl:0`, AMD Ryzen AI 9 HX 370) is readable after the `chmod` (resets at
  reboot). Readings are non-zero and monotonic; 20 s with the desktop idle: 9.5 W (8.6-10.6 J per second).
- The run's compute energy is the package energy of the timed section only. Per-frame power is noisy
  (33 ms intervals); plots should average over about 1 s.
- The minimum dwell time now also applies to the thermal-priority switch (it chattered frame to frame
  around tau_Q without it).
- Development runs, tuning sequence, `fog_severe`, one repeat, sensor power counted as 0 W, governor
  `powersave`, desktop and a VirtualBox VM running:

| Configuration | E0 (J) | Compute energy (J) | Mean power (W) | Time at L0 / L1 / L2 | ATE (m) | Losses |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| static_fusion | - | 2935 | 33.0 | 100 / 0 / 0 % | 0.017 | 0 |
| adaptive | 4000 | 2843 | 32.0 | 55 / 43 / 2 % | 0.022 | 0 |
| adaptive | 1500 | n/a (accounting bug, fixed) | - | 20 / 16 / 64 % | 4.77 | 1 |

- **Measured saving is small.** Mean package power by level in the adaptive run: 32.6 W (L0), 31.3 W (L1),
  29.7 W (L2), i.e. about 4 % and 9 % below Level 0 (5 % and 12 % after subtracting 9.5 W idle). Over the

  43 % is not supported by these measurements.
- **Level 2 in severe fog lost tracking** (E0 = 1500 J run): 500 features split between modalities while RGB
  is fogged; the map was lost and re-created later. One run, not a statistic, but it is the failure mode to
  watch during tuning.

# Tuning result (M7, D5), 2026-10-09 - PAPER

Run under the D13 conditions (governor `performance`, AC, VM off; Firefox, WeChat and OrcaSlicer were open and
idle). 180 runs on `office_loop_tune`, 7 h. Idle package power measured before the batch: 18.4 W (it was
9.4 W under `powersave` during development; the idle-subtracted figures depend on this).

**No setting met the constraints.** Following the D5 rule (fewest violations, then lowest energy), the selected
and frozen setting is `q0.6_e0.5-0.25_f800-700_k30-60_ba1_off1_s2`:
tau_Q 0.6; tau_E1 0.5, tau_E2 0.25; Level 1: 800 features, keyframe interval 30 frames, local BA every keyframe;
Level 2: 700 features, keyframe interval 60 frames, local BA every keyframe, **every 2nd frame processed**;
thermal-off rule on. `config/frozen.yaml` has `constraints_met: false`.

Stage 3 means over 3 repeats, tight budget (static fusion = reference, 2-3 repeats):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| clear | 0.0070 | 0.0077 | 0.0076 | 2740 | 2178 | 1.00 |
| fog_moderate | 0.0273 | 0.0301 | 0.0261 | 2651 | 2245 | 1.00 |
| fog_severe | 0.0180 | 0.0198 | **0.0275 (violation)** | 2684 | 2259 | 1.00 |
| lowlight | 0.0259 | 0.0285 | 0.0275 | 2615 | 2188 | 1.00 |

- Measured compute-energy saving against static fusion, summed over the four profiles: **17.0 %**
  (8870 J against 10690 J). The sensor term falls from 232 J to 207-217 J per run (thermal on 54 % of the time
  in clear, 71-72 % in the degraded profiles). Time at Level 0 / 1 / 2 is about 52 / 29 / 19 %.
- **The selected setting uses the reduced processing rate at Level 2** (D5: this changes the method text,
  which says the sensing rate is not throttled). The author has to be told.
- The ATE constraint is tighter than the run-to-run spread. Static fusion's own repeats give 0.010 / 0.024 /

  0.022 / 0.029 / 0.032 m (and 0.040 m in stage 2). With 2-3 repeats, a 10 % margin on a 2 cm reference cannot

- Stage 1: all three tau_Q values violated the ATE constraint in clear (0.023-0.028 m against 0.0077 m) with the
  initial levels; 0.6 had the fewest violations (2). Stage 2: 4 of 30 settings had one violation, none had zero.

  (about 50 %, a new map after a loss).

## Crashed runs and a fix to ORB-SLAM3 (before evaluation)

6 of the 180 tuning runs (3 %) ended without a result: 2 static-fusion reference runs (lowlight, fog_moderate)
and 4 stage-2 runs (lowlight x2, fog_moderate, fog_severe). None was in clear conditions. They are in
`tuning/log.csv` with `completed = False`. The tuner counted a crashed run as a constraint violation.
- Effect on the tuning: the lowlight and fog_moderate references rest on 2 repeats instead of 3. Three of the
  four crashed settings already had 2-3 violations in their completed profiles. The fourth
  (`f600-600_k15-30_ba2_off0_s1`) had one; its three completed profiles sum to 7338 J, so it would have needed
  under 1910 J in lowlight to reach the top three (no run was below 2100 J). The selection is unaffected.
- Cause (from the core dump of the first one): SIGABRT in the loop-closing thread,
  `LoopClosing::DetectCommonRegionsFromBoW -> Sim3Solver::iterate -> Sim3Solver::ComputeSim3`. Stock ORB-SLAM3
  divides the quaternion's imaginary part by its norm; for a degenerate RANSAC sample the norm is zero, the
  angle-axis vector is NaN and `Sophus::SO3f::exp` aborts the process. This is stock code, not part of the
  multispectral changes, and affects every configuration.
- Fix (in `orbslam3_ms.patch`, `ORBSLAM3_CHANGES.md` section 6): when the norm is zero or not finite, use the
  identity rotation (the correct limit for zero rotation) and let RANSAC score the hypothesis as usual. A run
  that did not crash never reached this branch, so completed tuning runs are unaffected and the tuning was not
  repeated. The change was made after tuning and before any evaluation run; `frozen.yaml` records the commit
  at which tuning ran (`31fb276`), the evaluation runs record the commit that contains the fix.
- The tuner did not keep the output of failed runs (`run_matrix` does, one log file per run). Any crash in the
  evaluation batches is reported as a failed run, not re-run silently.

# Main evaluation matrix, 2026-10-09/10 - first findings, OPEN (author decision needed)

Four batches with `config/frozen.yaml` at commit `5c8c0d7`: `runs/batch_20261008T235122Z` (office_loop_01),
`batch_20261009T071514Z` (office_crossover_01), `batch_20261009T143904Z` (corridor_tunnel_01),
`batch_20261009T230653Z` (industrial_hall_01). 400 runs, 399 completed. Nothing below has been filtered.

**1. False loop closures caused by repeated wall textures (simulation artefact).** 109 of 400 runs have an ATE
above 0.5 m, most of them with tracking intact. Example `office_loop_01`, clear, static RGB, repeat 0: the
trajectory is within 0.026 m of ground truth for 150 s; at t = 160.3 s (robot at (-5.51, 0.22)) a loop closure
moves the estimate to (5.40, 0.08), the mirror position on the opposite wall, where the camera had seen the
*same wall texture tile* at t = 70.8 s. `sim/worldgen/build_worlds.py` draws each 2 m wall segment's texture
from a family of only 12 images, so identical posters and marks occur at several places in every world. Place
recognition and the geometric check both pass on an identical tile. The tuning route did not expose it (static
fusion ATE 7 mm there).
- Runs with ATE > 0.5 m, of 20 per sequence (office_loop / crossover / corridor / hall): static RGB 15 / 14 /
  10 / 3; static fusion 1 / 5 / 10 / 0; adaptive generous 20 / 20 / 10 / 0; adaptive tight 1 / 0 / 0 / 0;
  static thermal 0 everywhere.
- It does not hit configurations evenly: adaptive at the tight budget is almost immune because Levels 1-2 run
  loop closure in reduced mode or off. That is an accidental advantage, not a merit of the method, so the
  accuracy comparison in these batches is not usable for the paper as it stands.
- The Sim3 guard added before the evaluation cannot create such a closure by itself (a hypothesis still needs
  the inlier support of matched points), but it has not been tested in isolation.

**2. Adaptive at the tight budget often fails the success criterion on the evaluation sequences.** Runs with
less than 90 % of frames in the main map: 15 / 15 / 0 / 16 of 20 (office_loop / crossover / corridor / hall).
Tracked fraction is typically 0.80-0.90 with 1-2 tracking losses and a new map. On the tuning sequence the same
setting tracked 100 %. This is an unfavourable result for the frozen setting and has to be reported.

(office_loop), -14.5 % (crossover), -13.1 % (corridor), -12.7 % (hall); generous budget -1.8 %, -2.2 %,

**4. Other.** `office_crossover_01` lowlight: every configuration fails (tracked about 0.5), static thermal
